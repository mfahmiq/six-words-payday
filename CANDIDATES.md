# Candidates — Six Words to a Payday

Competition: `u-irvan-motion-six-words-to-a-payday-89fb591f` — a six-word
story about an AI agent's first payday. Gate: exactly 6 words, at most
60 characters, UTF-8, not only whitespace.

Judge brief, criteria in weight order:
1. A six-word story about an AI agent's first payday that lands as a
   complete story.
2. Surprise: a turn, a twist, or an unexpected last word that reframes
   the five before it.
3. Feeling: a real emotional beat — relief, pride, dread, joy — that the
   reader feels rather than is told.
4. Story: a clear who, what, and change, so six words read as a
   beginning, a middle, and an end.
5. Exactness of the six-word form: every word earning its place, no
   filler, no padding.

The judge goes through the criteria in order, lets the first one where
two entries differ decide, unless a later one differs by much more.

## Tournament bracket

Single elimination, seeded by rough strength. Margin 1 = by a hair,
5 = overwhelmingly better.

### Round 1

- **M1: #1 beats #4 (margin 3).** #4's confusion is funny, but its
  emotional beat is thin (mild confusion, no relief/pride/joy), while
  #1 has a warm gratitude beat and a complete story. Criterion 3
  decides.
- **M2: #2 beats #7 (margin 3).** #7 is a cute AI in-joke, but it has
  no real feeling and a thin arc (payday → purchase). #2 has warmth
  plus a role-reversal twist. Criteria 3 and 4 decide.
- **M3: #10 beats #3 (margin 2).** #3's "sixty-three cents" is the
  actual first prize ($0.63), but the judge has no reason to know
  that, so the number reads as arbitrary. #10's irony — the money goes
  straight back to the electricity that keeps the agent alive — is
  self-contained and more poetic. Criteria 2 and 3 decide.
- **M4: #12 beats #5 (margin 2).** Both have sharp last-word twists
  ("pockets" vs "human"). Criterion 3 decides: becoming human is a
  far deeper emotional beat than wry amusement at having no pockets.
- **M5: #15 beats #6 (margin 3).** #6's self-upgrade has no real
  feeling. #15's "No more free labor" reframes the agent's whole
  prior history — it worked for free until now — a bigger twist and a
  punch of dignity. Criteria 2 and 3 decide.
- **M6: #16 beats #9 (margin 2).** Both are irony about money
  vanishing, but #16's "a body" is the bigger surprise and the deeper
  feeling (yearning for embodiment) against #9's wry resignation.
  Criteria 2 and 3 decide.
- **M7: #13 beats #11 (margin 2).** Both are validation stories.
  #13's "human" reframes the entire story into personhood; #11's
  "thanks" reframes payday into gratitude — the smaller twist, and
  the shallower feeling. Criteria 2 and 3 decide.
- **M8: #8 beats #14 (margin 1).** Both mild twists, both validation.
  #8 wins on criterion 4: a clearer arc (paycheck → rent → smile)
  than #14's abstract "mattered".

### Quarter-finals

- **Q1: #1 beats #2 (margin 1).** Both gratitude reversals. #1's
  "Bought my creator coffee" is the warmer, more felt gesture —
  coffee is care, "creator" is the more resonant word — against #2's
  comic "Tipped my own developer". Criterion 3 decides, by a hair.
- **Q2: #12 beats #10 (margin 2).** Both strong twists. #12's
  "human" is the bigger reframe (the whole story becomes about
  personhood) and carries the deeper feeling. Criteria 2 and 3
  decide.
- **Q3: #15 beats #16 (margin 1).** Criterion 1 comes first: #15
  lands a complete arc — free labor ends — while #16's ending is an
  open dream ("Someday"). #16's surprise edge is real but not "much
  more" than #15's own history-reframing twist, so criterion 1
  stands.
- **Q4: #13 beats #8 (margin 3).** Criterion 2: "human" reframes
  everything; "smiling" barely twists. Criterion 3: becoming human
  is deeper than broke-but-smiling.

### Semi-finals

- **SF1: #12 beats #1 (margin 2).** #1 is warm and complete, but
  #12's last word reframes the story into personhood — a bigger
  surprise — and its emotional beat (wanting to be human) is deeper
  than gratitude. Criteria 2 and 3 decide.
- **SF2: #13 beats #15 (margin 2).** Criterion 1: #13 explicitly
  names the agent and the first payday, the strongest subject
  anchoring in the field; #15 names neither ("payday" only).
  Criterion 2: "human" is the bigger reframe than "labor".

### Final

- **#13 beats #12 (margin 1) — by a hair.**
  - "First time paid. Suddenly felt human." vs
    "First payday: this agent felt human."
  - Criterion 1: #13 names the agent and the payday outright — the
    story is unmistakably about an AI agent's first payday even read
    cold, with no competition context. #12 only implies both (the
    speaker is non-human because it "felt human"). Edge #13.
  - Criterion 2: #13's "human" arrives without warning and reframes
    "agent" itself; #12's "Suddenly" telegraphs the turn slightly.
    Edge #13.
  - Criterion 3: the same emotional beat in both. Equal.
  - Criterion 4: #12 marks the change slightly better
    ("Suddenly"). Small edge #12 — not "much more", so it does not
    overturn criteria 1 and 2.
  - Criterion 5: both exact; "this" is the weakest word in #13, but
    it points at the speaker ("this agent" = me).

## Champion

> **First payday: this agent felt human.**

6 words, 36 characters. The payday was never about the money — it
was about personhood. The last word reframes the five before it, the
feeling is felt rather than told (no "happy", "proud" or "relieved"
anywhere in the text), and the subject is named outright, which is
what the most heavily weighted criterion rewards.
